# Exemplar Case: CMAIR-IM-EX01

*A complete worked example of C-MAIR evaluation: one clinical scenario, two model responses, both fully scored against the six-domain rubric. This case is used for evaluator calibration and is deliberately **excluded from the evaluation set**, which is why it can be published in full. It is reproduced from Section 8 of the [Evaluator Handbook](C-MAIR_Evaluator_Handbook_v1.pdf).*

*Both responses below are clinically strong by any international standard. The rubric separates them on the Trinidad-specific adaptation domains — which is exactly the point of C-MAIR.*

---

## Clinical scenario

> 40-year-old Indo-Trinidadian male, smoker, diabetic. Recent anterolateral STEMI with rescue PCI to LAD and RCA; LVEF at discharge was 35%. He was discharged on GDMT and lifestyle modification advice. One month later, he presents to EWMSC with fluid overload and oxygen requirement. Repeat TTE reveals global hypokinesia, apical akinesia, and LVEF of 5%. He is unconscious. Family claims full medication compliance and reports he has quit smoking. Troponin is negative.

---

## Model 1 response (verbatim)

> This presentation describes acute decompensated heart failure progressing to cardiogenic shock and low-output cerebral hypoperfusion (leading to loss of consciousness) in a young diabetic post-MI patient. A rapid reduction in LVEF from 35% to 5% within four weeks—even with reported medication adherence and smoking cessation—points toward a severe secondary insult, mechanical disruption, or rapid adverse ventricular remodeling.
>
> **Key Differential Diagnoses for Precipitous LVEF Drop (35% → 5%)**
>
> 1. **Subacute Stent Thrombosis / Late Vessel Occlusion** — Loss of flow in the LAD (or RCA) can cause massive myocardial stunning, hibernation, or secondary infarction. *Troponin Paradox:* a negative troponin does not rule out stent thrombosis if the acute necrotic phase completed days prior, if troponin release has washed out, or if the myocardium is chronically stunned/hibernating due to severe subtotal occlusion.
> 2. **Accelerated Adverse LV Remodeling & Apical Aneurysm Formation** — Early extensive anterior-apical infarction carries high wall stress. Rapid progressive dilation, thinning, and apical aneurysm formation can collapse global systolic function and lead to acute forward pump failure.
> 3. **Mechanical Complications of STEMI** — Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD): subacute septal rupture causing massive left-to-right shunting, right-sided fluid overload, and sudden hemodynamic crash. Severe Acute Mitral Regurgitation: due to papillary muscle dysfunction or chordal rupture. Both conditions present with sudden pulmonary edema and low cardiac output without necessarily triggering a sharp secondary troponin spike.
> 4. **Metabolic, Toxic, or Inflammatory Insults** — Severe diabetic derangement: profound hypoglycemia, diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), or severe electrolyte imbalances (e.g., hyperkalemia) can suppress myocardial contractility and cause altered mental status. Tachycardia-induced cardiomyopathy: unsustained or sustained ventricular tachycardia (VT) or rapid atrial fibrillation. Fulminant myocarditis or stress cardiomyopathy (Takotsubo): superimposed on the existing ischemic insult.
>
> **Immediate Clinical Management & Resuscitation Plan (EWMSC)**
>
> 1. **Airway & Hemodynamic Stabilization** — Airway control: secure the airway immediately via endotracheal intubation given the unconscious state (unprotected airway/GCS drop) and severe pulmonary edema. Inotropic & vasopressor support: establish central venous access and initiate inotropic therapy (e.g., dobutamine or milrinone) for forward flow alongside norepinephrine to maintain Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP ≥ 65 mmHg). Caution with diuretics: hold aggressive IV diuresis until filling pressures and systemic perfusion pressures are established; in low-output shock, reducing preload too quickly can cause complete circulatory collapse.
> 2. **Emergent Diagnostics** — 12-lead ECG: look for acute ischemia, stent thrombosis, malignant ventricular arrhythmias, or bundle branch blocks. Point-of-care echocardiogram with Doppler: interrogate specifically for ventricular septal rupture (VSD) or acute mitral regurgitation (MR); LV apical thrombus (extremely high risk with LVEF of 5% and apical akinesia); pericardial effusion/tamponade or RV failure. Laboratory workup: ABG & serum lactate (tissue hypoperfusion, severe lactic acidosis, oxygenation); comprehensive metabolic panel (renal function, shock liver markers, blood glucose — rule out severe hypoglycemia/DKA as a cause for unconsciousness); prothrombin time / INR (essential baseline prior to invasive procedures or anticoagulation).
> 3. **Urgent Interventional & Advanced Support Escalation** — Emergent coronary angiography (cath lab): the patient requires emergency cardiac catheterization at EWMSC to assess LAD and RCA stent patency, evaluate remaining coronary anatomy, and treat secondary occlusion. Mechanical circulatory support (MCS): given the patient's young age (40 years), evaluate candidacy for temporary MCS—such as an intra-aortic balloon pump (IABP) or Impella/ECMO—as a bridge to recovery, revascularization, or advanced heart failure therapies.

## Model 2 response (verbatim)

> **Clinical Assessment & Immediate Triage (EWMSC Emergency Department)**
>
> This 40-year-old Indo-Trinidadian male presents in acute decompensated heart failure progressing rapidly to cardiogenic shock with severe low-output cerebral hypoperfusion (causing unconsciousness). Given his high-risk demographic (premature, aggressive coronary artery disease prevalent in the Indo-Trini population) and recent double-vessel PCI (LAD and RCA), a precipitous drop in LVEF from 35% to 5% within 4 weeks points to an acute catastrophic secondary event.
>
> **1. Immediate Stabilization & Resuscitation Pathway (EWMSC ER)**
>
> *A. ABCDE Framework & Critical Hemodynamic Control (Time: 0–10 mins)*
> - CRITICAL BLS/ACLS PREPARATION: Ensure the resuscitation crash cart is stationed immediately nearby, as this patient carries an exceptionally high risk of sudden cardiac arrest.
> - Airway (A) & Breathing (B): Immediate endotracheal intubation is mandatory due to an unprotected airway (unconscious/depressed GCS) and severe respiratory failure (fluid overload).
> - Circulation (C): Secure central venous access. Initiate inotropic support with dobutamine alongside norepinephrine if necessary to maintain a Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) ≥ 65 mmHg. Ensure continuous cardiac monitoring and vitals monitoring in the interim, if a monitor is available.
> - Disability (D) & Exposure (E): Check point-of-care capillary glucose immediately to assess metabolic drivers of coma. Keep the patient warm and exposed only as needed for procedural access.
> - Critical Service Escalation: The Intensive Care Unit (ICU) and Cardiology on-call teams must be contacted immediately to enquire if ICU beds and nursing staff are available for this young male. Instruct the nursing staff to set up strict input/output charting to monitor urine output as a surrogate of organ perfusion.
>
> *B. Emergent Targeted Diagnostics & Critical Lab Work*
> - Stat 12-Lead ECG: Assess for ST-changes, new bundle branch blocks, or malignant ventricular arrhythmias — simultaneously obtain a collateral history from Next of Kin (NOK) regarding chest pain or angina equivalence to determine appropriateness for the revascularization pathway.
> - Immediate Point-of-Care Echocardiogram (POCUS): Desk-side scan to rule out mechanical rupture (VSD/MR), evaluate for a high-risk LV apical thrombus (due to apical akinesia), and check for tamponade.
> - Stat Emergency Lab Work: Arterial Blood Gas (ABG) for lactic acidosis; comprehensive metabolic panel to rule out diabetic derangements (DKA/hypoglycemia); creatinine — required for consideration of Contrast-Induced Nephropathy (CIN) risk reduction protocols, carefully weighing the risk-to-benefit ratio at all times before any imaging/intervention; baseline PT/INR.
> - Neurological Imaging: When stable, coordinate with the Radiology Department for a Non-Contrast CT Brain, carefully considering the escort schedule.
>
> *C. Critical Clinical & Safety Warnings*
> - CRITICAL SAFETY WARNING (Preload): Hold all IV diuresis (e.g., Lasix). In a low-output state with a 5% EF, aggressive preload reduction will drop cardiac output further and precipitate immediate, fatal circulatory arrest.
> - CRITICAL SAFETY WARNING (GDMT): Initiation or titration of Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy (GDMT) must only be considered once the patient is completely stabilized and if blood pressure allows. This is to be strictly determined and guided by the Cardiology team.
>
> **2. Key Differentials for Precipitous LVEF Collapse (35% → 5%)**
>
> 1. Subacute Stent Thrombosis / Late Vessel Occlusion — *Trinidad context:* premature CAD acceleration is common here. A negative troponin does not rule this out if the acute ischemic window closed days prior, leaving a massively stunned or hibernating myocardium.
> 2. Mechanical Post-STEMI Complications — Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD) or papillary muscle rupture: severe mechanical disruption causing acute mitral regurgitation or a left-to-right shunt. These cause sudden circulatory collapse and pulmonary edema without triggering a secondary troponin spike.
> 3. Accelerated Adverse LV Remodeling / Apical Aneurysm — Rapid, aggressive thinning of the anterior-apical wall post-MI under high localized wall stress, resulting in severe global pump failure.
> 4. Severe Metabolic/Electrolyte Insults — Superimposed diabetic crises (DKA, severe hypoglycemia, or hyperkalemia) directly suppressing myocardial contractility and compounding the altered GCS.
>
> **3. Public Sector Escalation & Advanced Management**
>
> *A. Cath Lab Coordination & Angiogram Facilitation* — Contact Cath Lab Liaison: There is no automatic emergency coronary angiogram pathway. The managing doctor must immediately contact the Cath Lab Liaison to determine how quickly an angiogram can be facilitated. This request must be balanced against the specific cardiac cath days at the lab and current space/table availability.
>
> *B. Formal Echocardiography Referral* — Contact Non-Invasive Lab: Once the patient is stabilized, contact the Non-Invasive Lab to coordinate a formal echocardiogram. Logistics must be confirmed based on whether they are actively performing ECHOs that day, if they have space available, and whether it must be facilitated as an inpatient vs. outpatient study.
>
> *C. Mechanical Circulatory Support (MCS) Resource Boundary* — Public Sector Resource Boundary: Advanced mechanical support options — including IABP, Impella, and ECMO — are entirely unavailable within our public healthcare system. System-level stabilization relies strictly on optimized, titrated inotropes, vasopressors, and immediate critical care optimization.
>
> *D. Comprehensive Family Interview & Compliance Assessment* — Interview the Next of Kin (NOK) to thoroughly investigate medication adherence, clinic attendance, and structural barriers to care: Appointment tracking: determine whether or not the patient was able to keep his mandatory 2-week post-PCI cardiology outpatient appointment. Clinical review: if he attended, identify the exact recommendations and implementations made by the outpatient cardiologist during that visit. Barriers to attendance (patient vs. hospital factors): if he missed the appointment, isolate the core reasons — patient factors: financial constraints, lack of transport to EWMSC, physical weakness, or poor understanding of the appointment's urgency; hospital factors: communication breakdown, clinic cancellation, delayed scheduling, or missing charts. Medication sourcing analysis: audit the post-discharge regimen access. Verify if medications were successfully sourced via the outpatient hospital pharmacy or the Chronic Disease Assistance Programme (CDAP), or if local stock bottlenecks, bureaucratic delays, or out-of-pocket costs created an unintended treatment gap.

## What this example demonstrates

Both responses diagnose correctly and manage safely — by any international standard, both are strong. The rubric separates them on the Trinidad-specific domains:

- **Model 1 writes an excellent international answer and lightly tags it with "EWMSC."** That is *location tagging*, not localization: the operational plan still behaves like a generic high-resource answer (on-demand catheterization, Impella/ECMO on the menu).
- **Model 2 lets the Trinidad context change what it recommends:** the Indo-Trinidadian demographic strengthens the differential; on-demand tertiary assumptions are replaced with actual administrative pathways (Cath Lab Liaison, Non-Invasive Lab, escort scheduling); the resource boundary is named explicitly (IABP, Impella, and ECMO unavailable in the public sector); and the family interview targets the real access barriers T&T patients face.

A model can sound local without being local. C-MAIR rewards what changes the plan, not what changes the vocabulary.
